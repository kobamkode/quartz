## Naming words (Nouns):

>These are people, places, animals, or things.

cat, mom, park, cookie, happiness

## Action words (Verbs)

>These are what you DO.

run, jump, eat, sleep, think

## Describing words (Adjectives)

>These tell us MORE about naming words - what they're like!

big dog, red ball, happy kid, yucky broccoli

## Describing action words (Adverbs)

>These tell us HOW you do something.

run quickly, speak softly, laugh loudly

## Connecting words

These glue sentences together!

>and, but, because, or

- "I like ice cream AND cookies"
- "I'm tired BECAUSE I played all day"

## Fancier connecting words

>Fancy "And" glue:
>moreover, furthermore, additionally, besides

- "I like pizza. MOREOVER, I think it's the best food ever!"
(This is like saying "and also" but fancier)

>Fancy "But" glue:
>however, nevertheless, nonetheless, yet, although, though, whereas

- "I studied hard. HOWEVER, I still found the test difficult."
- "ALTHOUGH it was raining, we went outside." 
(These all mean "but" in different ways)

>Fancy "So" glue:
>therefore, thus, consequently, hence, accordingly

- "It was cold. THEREFORE, I wore a jacket."
(These all mean "so this happened because of that")

>Fancy "Or" glue:
>alternatively, otherwise

- "Finish your homework. OTHERWISE, you can't play."
(Otherwise = "or else this bad thing will happen")

>Fancy time glue:
>meanwhile, subsequently, eventually, ultimately

- "I cooked dinner. MEANWHILE, my sister set the table."
(Meanwhile = at the same time)

>Fancy example/explaining glue:
>namely, specifically, for instance, for example

- "I like fruit, SPECIFICALLY apples and oranges."

>The tricky part:
>Some fancy glue needs a semicolon (;) or period before it:

- "I was tired; however, I kept working."
- "I was tired. However, I kept working."

## When to use THE

>Use "THE" when you're talking about something SPECIFIC - something you and the listener both know about:

- "Can you close THE door?" (we both know which door - the one right here!)
- "I fed THE cat" (your cat, the one we both know)
- "Look at THE moon!" (there's only one moon, so we know which one)

>DON'T use "the" when you're talking about something general - any random one:

- "I want A cookie" (any cookie is fine, not a special one)
- "Dogs are cute" (all dogs in general, not one specific dog)
- "I like pizza" (pizza in general, not one specific pizza)

>Think of it like pointing:
> - "THE" = you're pointing at something specific ☝️
> - "A/AN" = you're just mentioning any random one 🤷

## Tenses

| Tense | Formula | Time | What it shows | example |
| --------------- | --------------- | --------------- | --------------- | --------------- |
| Past Perfect |  Subject + had + past participle  | 6am | Action BEFORE another past | I had eaten cookie (before brushing) |
| Past Continuous |  Subject + was/were + Verb-ing  | 7:30am | In progress in past | I was eating breakfast (when phone rang) |
| Past Simple |  Subject + Verb-ed  | 8am | Finished past action | I ate sandwich |
| Present Perfect |  Subject + has/have + past participle  | 8am -> now | Past affecting now | I have eaten (so I'm full now) |
| Present Simple |  Subject + Verb  | every day | Habit/routine | I eat lunch daily |
| Present Continuous |  Subject + am/is/are + Verb-ing  | now | Happening now | I am eating apple |
| Future Simple |  Subject + will + Verb  | 6pm | Will happen | I will eat dinner |
| Future Continuous |  Subject + will be + Verb-ing  |6 -> 6:30pm | Will be in progress | I will be eating at 6:15pm |
| Future Perfect |  Subject + will have + past participle  | before 9pm | Will finish before deadline | I will have eaten all meals by 9pm |


## What's a past participle?

It's the special form of the verb used with has/have/had:

Regular verbs: just add -ed (same as past simple)

- play → played
- walk → walked

Irregular verbs: these change (you have to memorize them!)

- eat → eaten
- go → gone
- see → seen
- write → written
- do → done
